# Fact idea engine: finding true stories that work as animated shorts

## What makes a topic travel
A good topic has all four. Score each pitch 1-5 on each and show the scores, so the user sees *why*.
1. **A paradox in one line:** it sounds wrong, dumb or impossible, and it's true.
2. **Stakes + people:** someone (an everyman or a real person) did something, and something was at risk: money, a
   deadline, pride, a law, a life.
3. **Visual cause and effect:** you can *show* it happening. Physical stories (food, machines, weather, animals,
   inventions, collapses) animate far better than abstract ones (statistics, policy, pure psychology).
4. **One idea to explain it,** ideally with an everyday analogy. If it takes three ideas, it's too big for 60 s.

## Where to dig (filtered by the user's interests)
- **A trick or practice people really use** (the strongest vein today): kitchen tests, trade secrets, old military and
  sailing practices, engineering rules of thumb, shop and casino habits. Tell it as a parable: an unnamed "he" does the
  strange thing, everyone stares, the real reason pays off.
- Science with a body feeling: heat, cold, pressure, sound, light, balance, hunger, sleep.
- "Accidental" history: inventions found by mistake, disasters with absurd causes, rules created by one incident.
- Animals and nature doing "impossible" things.
- Laws and rules that exist because of one weird event, or a loophole.
- Everyday objects with a surprising reason for their shape.
- "If you did X, Y would happen" thought experiments with real physics (Fact explainer).

## Turning a mechanism into a "He did …" hook
Find the moment someone does the thing that only makes sense once you know the mechanism. That's the hook, in past
tense, without the reason; the mechanism answers the mystery beat later. (Original examples:)
- Mechanism: a raw egg's liquid inside drags when it spins, a boiled egg's solid inside doesn't → deed: **"He spun
  every egg in the diner before he cracked a single one."**
- Mechanism: hot water makes a metal lid expand more than the glass jar → deed: **"He fixed the stuck jam jar by
  giving it a bath."**
- Mechanism: salt makes an ice bath colder than plain ice → deed: **"He poured a whole bag of salt into the drinks
  cooler, ten minutes before the party."**

Check every mechanism against two good sources before writing.

## Pitch format (6 pitches, one message)
Mark each pitch **parable** (an invented everyman around a sourced mechanism), **real event** (every detail sourced)
or **explainer**, and give its hook in the "He did …" shape unless it's an explainer.

```
### 1. The Cook Who Spun Every Egg · parable · paradox 4 · stakes 4 · visual 5 · explain 5
Hook: "He spun every egg in the diner before he cracked a single one."
Mechanism: the spin test (boiled = solid, turns as one; raw = liquid, drags and restarts after a stop); a fast-spun
boiled egg rises onto its tip.
Why it works: a tiny act, a busy kitchen, a satisfying test anyone can try, a big visual ending.
Picture: the spinning egg, a crate "BOILED + RAW", a chalkboard cross-section, the egg standing up.
Verify: Scientific American / Science Buddies (the spin test); Moffatt & Shimomura, Nature 2002 (the rising egg).
```
Rank them, say which one you'd make first and why, and let the user pick, mix or ask for more in a direction.

## Red flags (skip or warn)
- The only source is a viral post, a listicle or another short ("fact" chains often trace back to nothing).
- It needs graphic violence, real living private people, or medical or financial advice to work.
- Its whole appeal is somebody else's video. Find a different angle or topic.
