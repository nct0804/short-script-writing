# Moral Fables: teaching a value without preaching

A **moral Fable** is a Fable story (or gag) built around one value (honesty, kindness, courage, patience, sharing,
gratitude, forgiveness) so the viewer *feels* the lesson. It keeps every measured pattern of the Fable: sentence 1 is
the rule, a kid narrator who doesn't fit, a loved one with a ritual, the turn, a sacrifice, a callback, 4.3 words/s,
~6-word sentences. The moral is never a separate part of the video. It is the rule, seen from the other side.

## Why this fits the research
- In the study set, the strongest long stories already turn on a value: the misfit narrator **does** something brave,
  kind or selfless *through* the rule, and it costs them. Several are built directly on a kindness or karma rule.
- The lesson is carried by the **sacrifice** and the **callback**, not by a sentence that explains it. "No narrating
  feelings until the reveal" applies to morals too.
- Endings that turn to the viewer (a call to action, a "part 2" cliffhanger) were the weakest. A spoken moral ("and
  that's why we should always …") is the same mistake: it steps out of the story to talk at the viewer.

## The method: value → action → rule
1. **Pick one value.** One per story. Two values blur both.
2. **Find the everyday action where that value is tested:** telling a lie, saying something mean, rushing someone,
   keeping the last cookie, staying quiet when someone is picked on.
3. **Make the rule show the opposite of the value, visibly:** the lie makes your shadow walk away; the mean word turns
   into a stone in your own backpack. Frame 1 shows it.
4. **The misfit already has the value too much** (too honest to lie, too slow to get angry, gives everything away),
   and it's a joke in beat 3 ("The kids call me Glue.").
5. **The pressure is the whole town failing the value,** adults included, and paying for it.
6. **The turn re-reads the rule, in story words, not life words:** "A shadow doesn't leave when you lie. It goes to
   wait for the truth." That line *is* the moral, said about the world of the story.
7. **The sacrifice is the narrator doing the value at a cost:** the hardest true thing, the kind word to the worst
   person, the last cookie given away.
8. **The callback shows the value changed someone:** the ritual, changed. Nothing after it.

## Original value → rule ideas
| Value | Everyday action | Rule (original) |
|---|---|---|
| Honesty | telling a lie | "Every time you tell a lie, your shadow takes one step away from you." (worked example in `examples.md`) |
| Kindness | saying something mean | "Every unkind word you say drops into your own backpack as a stone." |
| Courage | staying quiet when someone is picked on | "In my town, if you stay quiet when someone needs you, you turn a little grey." |
| Patience | rushing someone | "Every time you hurry someone, the clocks steal a minute of your day." |
| Sharing | keeping something all to yourself | "Food you share tastes twice as good. Food you hide tastes of nothing at all." |
| Gratitude | forgetting to say thank you | "Every thank-you you never say follows you around as a little rain cloud." |
| Forgiveness | holding a grudge | "In this town, a grudge grows on your back like ivy, and it never stops growing." |
| Listening | interrupting | "Interrupt someone, and you lose the next word you were going to say. Forever." |
| Humility | bragging | "Every brag makes you one inch taller. The proudest kid in school can't fit through the door." |

Run each one through the four tests in `idea-engine.md` (everyday action, visible in frame 1, a price, a loophole)
before writing.

## Where the moral lives in the 8 beats
| Beat | The moral's job |
|---|---|
| 1. The rule | the value's opposite, made visible (never the value's name) |
| 2. Proof montage | funny proofs of what the town loses by failing the value; plant where lost things go |
| 3. Me? | the narrator has the value too much, played as a joke |
| 4. Loved one + ritual | the loved one is quietly failing the value (a kind lie, a grudge, a silence) |
| 5. Pressure | everyone fails it at once; the cost becomes dangerous |
| 6. The turn | the rule re-read: the moral, in story words |
| 7. Sacrifice | the narrator acts the value out, and it costs |
| 8. Callback | the ritual changed by the value; the viewer finishes the lesson alone |

## Moral gags (36 s)
The flip carries the lesson as a laugh. *When Pigs Fly* (in `examples.md`) is a moral gag: trying too hard to impress
someone gets the pig the date. Keep the shape: premise, three escalations, a one-line flip. Never explain the joke.

## Rules
- **Never say the moral.** No "the moral of the story", no "and that's why we should …", no adult explaining it, no
  naming the value ("honesty", "kindness") in the narration. Show it.
- **The title names the rule, not the lesson:** MY SHADOW LEFT WITHOUT ME…, not WHY HONESTY MATTERS.
- **The narrator is never punished for having the value;** the cost comes from choosing it when it's hard.
- **Kid-safe,** and no religious or political moralizing unless the user asks for it.
- **For teachers and parents:** after the script, add a one-line **teacher note** (not spoken): the value in one
  sentence and one question to ask a child after watching. It never goes into the narration.
