# MY LITTLE BROTHER WON'T LET GO OF HIS BALLOON...

**Type:** Fiction · **Format:** Fiction story (warm) · **Target length:** about 1:45 · **Words:** 452 (≈4.3 words/s): 48 / 101 / 33 / 59 / 69 / 48 / 64 / 30 per beat
**Logline:** In a town where letting go of a balloon erases a memory, the clumsiest kid in town saves his little brother from floating away by letting go for him.
**Title options:** 1. MY LITTLE BROTHER WON'T LET GO OF HIS BALLOON... (recommended) · 2. EVERY BALLOON TAKES A MEMORY · 3. THE CLOUD THAT KEEPS EVERYTHING

## Script

### 1. The rule · 0:00–0:11
In my town, when you let go of a balloon, it carries one memory away with it. You see, the higher it floats, the older the memory. Hold on too long, and it carries you instead. My little brother has been holding the same balloon for 312 days.

### 2. Proof montage · 0:11–0:35
Mayor Dunn let go of three balloons at the summer fair. He still doesn't know he's married. His wife is being very patient about it. Mrs. Petrov ties hers to her dog. The dog forgot how to sit. Our dentist gives out a balloon after every filling, so nobody remembers the pain. Or the bill. In 1979 the Kowalski twins let go of a whole bunch at once. They're fifty now. They still shake hands every morning. And above Harlow Hill there's a cloud that never moves. Every balloon ends up there. Nobody's been up. Nobody wants to forget the way.

### 3. Me? · 0:35–0:42
Me? I can't hold anything. Phones. Ice cream. Eggs. Mostly eggs. The kids call me Butterfingers. I've lost forty-one balloons. I don't remember what any of them took. That's sort of the point.

### 4. Loved one + ritual · 0:42–0:56
My brother Pip is five. Since last winter, he's held one red balloon. He eats with it. He sleeps with the string around his wrist. Every night he lifts it to the window and whispers, "Still here?" And I say, "Still here." Mom gave it to him at the hospital. The day before. He's never let go. Not once.

### 5. Pressure · 0:56–1:12
Then the balloon started winning. Monday, Pip's toes left the floor. Tuesday, his whole feet. Wednesday he floated to the ceiling during breakfast. Dad tied him to the kitchen table. The table floated too. "Pip, just let go," Dad said. Pip held on harder. Thursday, 4:12. Wind. The window. Dad grabbed his ankle. Too late. The mailman jumped. He's very short. Pip went up, and up, toward the cloud.

### 6. The rule turns around · 1:12–1:23
I climbed Harlow Hill faster than I've ever done anything. Up close, the cloud wasn't a cloud. It was a million balloons, every one glowing with a picture. Mayor Dunn's wedding. Two boys on a seesaw. That's when I understood. The sky doesn't take memories. It keeps them.

### 7. Sacrifice through the rule · 1:23–1:38
Pip hung at the edge, crying, holding on. If he let go, he'd forget her. Whoever lets go loses it. So I reached up. "Give it to me. I'm good at this." The string touched my hand. It slipped. Like always. Pip fell into my arms. The red balloon floated into the cloud. And Mom went with it. My memory of her. Not his.

### 8. Callback · 1:38–1:45
Every night, Pip lifts an empty hand to the window. "Still here?" "Still here." Then he tells me one thing about Mom. Tonight he said she couldn't hold anything either.

# WHEN PIGS FLY 🐷

**Type:** Fiction · **Format:** Fiction gag · **Words:** 184 (≈5.2 words/s)
**Logline:** A boy takes his crush's "when pigs fly" literally.

## Script

### Premise · 0:00–0:04
My crush said she'd go out with me when pigs fly. So I bought a pig.

### Escalation · 0:04–0:30
His name is Kevin. Kevin weighs two hundred pounds and hates heights. Day one, I tied forty balloons to him. Kevin ate eleven. Pop, pop, pop. Day nine, a trampoline. Kevin bounced once and broke it. The neighbours applauded anyway. Day fifteen, a catapult. Kevin flew four feet, landed in the pool, and refused to leave it for a week. Day twenty, I built wings out of cardboard and my mom's umbrellas. Kevin wore them proudly. He did not fly. He did get a fan club. Day thirty, I spent my entire college fund on a hot air balloon. Kevin finally flew. Over her house. Over her school. Right past her bedroom window, wearing a little tuxedo. She opened the window. She looked at him. She looked at me, hanging off the basket, screaming. Then she walked out to the field where we landed, with her phone out, and walked right past me.

### Flip · 0:30–0:36
She took a picture with Kevin. "Okay," she said. "But I'm going out with HIM."

# MY CRUSH'S LAUGH GROWS FLOWERS 🌷

**Type:** Fiction · **Format:** Fiction gag · **Words:** 182 (≈5.2 words/s)
**Logline:** A boy whose crush's laugh makes flowers grow spends thirty days failing to make her laugh, until a disaster
finally does, and now she wants it every day.

## Script

### Premise · 0:00–0:04 · 21 words
Every time my crush laughs, flowers grow wherever she looks. I've known her four years. I've never grown a single flower.

### Escalation · 0:04–0:33 · 146 words
Day one, I told her my best joke. Why did the scarecrow win an award? She blinked. One weed came up. It was brown.
Day nine, I wore my dad's clown nose and a squeaky shoe to class. She sighed. The cactus on her desk died. The janitor
buried it. Day fifteen, I paid Marcus, the funniest kid in fourth grade, three dollars a joke. Forty jokes. She laughed
once. At Marcus. Marcus got a rose bush. Day twenty, I learned to juggle. Three eggs. Then two eggs. Then I was wearing
them. Day thirty, the big one. Lunch, twelve fifteen. Banana peel, slime bucket, whoopee cushion, all timed to the
second. I slipped on the banana first. Into the slime. Onto the cushion. Pfffft. She laughed so hard the cafeteria
turned into a jungle. Two lunch ladies are still in there. We hear them sometimes.

### Flip · 0:33–0:36 · 15 words
She wiped her eyes and said, "Same time tomorrow?" Now flowers grow wherever I fall.

## Notes
- **Day cards carry the time jumps** (Day one … Day thirty): the escalation needs no "then, later" sentences.
- **Plants → payoffs:** the brown weed (day 1) → the jungle (day 30); his empty pot "MINE: 0" (hook) → flowers where he
  fell (last line).
- **Sound words are cut points:** "Pfffft." gets its own shot and its own sound.
- **The flip is two lines:** her question turns the premise around, then the last line echoes the first, changed.
- Animated, this read ran 45 s, not 36: the only child voice available read slowly. Pick a fast voice for gags
  (`course/05-from-script-to-video.md`).

## Captions
Top-of-frame 2-4 word chunks; keywords in green CAPS: FLOWERS, WEED, MARCUS, JUNGLE, PFFFT.

## Comment bait
