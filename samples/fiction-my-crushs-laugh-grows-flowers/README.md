# Sample · Fiction gag: My Crush's Laugh Grows Flowers 🌷

`final.mp4` (46 s, 1080x1920) · `script.md` (the full script with the world bible, shot list, captions, score)

## How it was made
1. **Script** with `fictional-script`. Seed: *"My crush's laugh makes flowers grow. She has never laughed at anything I
   say."*
   - The skill offered three rule versions and recommended *"Every time my crush laughs, flowers grow wherever she
     looks"*: the strangest frame-1 image, and ready for a callback.
   - It planned the plants and payoffs: the brown weed → the jungle; the empty pot "MINE: 0" → the flowers where he
     falls.
   - Score: 89/100.
2. **Voice:** the only true child voice in the library, sped up 1.25× with every pause trimmed: 44.9 s at 4.07
   words/s. The gag format wants 36 s at 5.2: see "What to learn".
3. **Picture:** a school yard, a classroom and a cafeteria that turns into a jungle; 16 shots, about 36 set-ups, a cut
   every ~1.3 s (the fast cut speed for bright comedy).
   - Day cards (DAY 1 / 9 / 15 / 20 / 30) carry the time jumps.
   - Every gag has a prop: the brown weed, the clown nose, the wilting cactus, "$3 / JOKE", the "40 JOKES" stack, the
     yolk, the banana peel, the slime bucket, the whoopee cushion.
   - Every shot and cut is timed from the spoken words, so a new voice take re-times the picture on its own.
4. **Captions:** 2-4 word chunks at the top of the frame, keywords in green CAPS.
5. **Sound:** a pop for every flower, a squeak, a sad trombone for the cactus, coins, a slide whistle for the slip, SPLAT
   and PFFFT, jungle birds, a record scratch on the flip, a jingle on the last word. Mastered at −14 LUFS.

## What to learn from it
- **Rule-of-three escalation with day cards** needs no "and then, later …" sentences.
- **Sound words are beats:** "Pfffft." gets its own shot, its own sound and its own caption.
- **The last line echoes the first, changed:** "flowers grow wherever she looks" → "flowers grow wherever I fall".
- **Pick the voice for the pace.** The read decides the length, and a slow voice turns a 36 s gag into a 46 s one. For
  gags, choose a naturally fast voice before you record.
