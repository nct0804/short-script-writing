# Lesson 3 · Writing a Fable script

A Fable makes the viewer accept an impossible rule in sentence 1, laugh at it for 30 seconds, then care about a kid
and someone small they love. Worked examples: *My Little Brother Won't Let Go of His Balloon…* (a warm story) and *My
Crush's Laugh Grows Flowers* (a gag, animated in `samples/fable-my-crushs-laugh-grows-flowers/`). Both are in
`.claude/skills/fictional-script/references/examples.md`.

## 1. Turn a seed into a rule
Anything can be a seed (a word, a feeling, a "what if"). Make it a rule that passes **four tests**:
1. **An everyday action** anyone does: talking, laughing, blinking, letting go of a balloon.
2. **Visible in frame 1:** you can picture the strangest image of the story as the very first shot.
3. **A price:** breaking or misusing the rule costs something big.
4. **A loophole** viewers will argue about in the comments.

> Seed: *balloons*. Rule: **"In my town, when you let go of a balloon, it carries one memory away with it."**
> Price: hold on too long, and it carries you instead. Loophole: why not just pop it?

## 2. The misfit and the heart
- **The misfit trait is the rule's blind spot.** A rule about holding on → a kid who can't hold anything
  ("Butterfingers"). The trait is a joke at 0:35 and the solution at 1:30.
- **A loved one with a ritual:** a little brother who lifts the balloon to the window every night: "Still here?"
  "Still here." The ritual comes back in the last line, changed.

## 3. The 8 beats (warm Fable story, ~2 min, ~520 words)
1. **The rule** (0-5 s) + the price + a personal stake ("He's held it for 312 days.")
2. **Proof montage** (5-30 s): 3-6 funny results, each with a name and a number. Plant one cold fact.
3. **"Me?"** The narrator doesn't fit.
4. **Loved one + ritual.**
5. **Pressure:** a countdown (Monday … Tuesday … Thursday, 4:12), adults fail.
6. **The turn:** "That's when I understood." The rule meant something else.
7. **Sacrifice through the rule:** the misfit trait saves them, and it costs.
8. **Callback:** the ritual, changed. Cut.

**Eerie variant (crisis-first):** rule → *tonight it broke* by sentence 6 → world facts that raise the danger → the one
who depends on the narrator → the turn → a costly choice → a calm dread sting. Present tense, slower cuts, a cinematic
look.

## 4. The Fable gag (36 s, ~185 words)
Premise → three escalations, each bigger → a one-line dialogue flip.
> "Every time my crush laughs, flowers grow wherever she looks. I've known her four years. I've never grown a single
> flower." → Day one, a joke: one brown weed. Day nine, a clown nose: the cactus dies. Day thirty: a banana peel, a
> slime bucket, a whoopee cushion: a jungle. → "Same time tomorrow?" → "Now flowers grow wherever I fall."

Day cards ("Day one … Day thirty") carry the time jumps for free. Sound words ("Pfffft.") get their own beat.

## 5. Moral Fables: teaching a value without preaching
A moral Fable keeps everything above, and builds the rule around **one value** (method:
`.claude/skills/fictional-script/references/morals.md`):
1. **Pick the value** (honesty) and **the everyday action that tests it** (telling a lie).
2. **The rule shows the value's opposite, visibly:** "Every time you tell a lie, your shadow takes one step away from
   you."
3. **The misfit has the value too much:** the kid who can't lie at all. The kids call him Glue.
4. **The loved one quietly fails it:** Grandma, who says "I'm fine, sweetheart" every night since Grandpa died.
5. **The turn re-reads the rule, in story words:** "A shadow doesn't leave when you lie. It goes to wait for the truth."
6. **The sacrifice acts the value out at a cost:** "You're not fine, Gran. You're sad. And I'm sad too." He makes her
   cry, and the shadows come running home.
7. **The callback:** the ritual, answered honestly this time. Nothing after it.

**Never say the moral:** no "the moral of the story", no lecture, no naming the value in the narration. The viewer
finishes the lesson alone, which is why it sticks. The full script, *My Shadow Left Without Me…*, is in the examples
file, with a teacher note (one sentence + one question) for classrooms and parents.

## 6. Sound like the viral ones
- **4.3 words/s** (gag 5.2). **~6-word sentences**, a one-word sentence every 8-10 lines ("Nothing." "Eggs.").
- **Numbers everywhere:** 312 days, 41 balloons, 4:12.
- **Deadpan jokes inside the danger:** "Dad tied him to the kitchen table. The table floated too."
- **No feelings narrated** until the turn. Show them: "He's never let go. Not once."

## 7. Ask Claude
- "Fable story about [seed]." → 2-3 rule versions, then the full script, world bible, shot list and score.
- "Fable gag: my crush [secret power or literal reading]."
- "Pitch me 5 Fables: one warm, one bittersweet, one eerie, two gags."
- "A moral Fable about sharing, for 6-year-olds."
- "Darker ending." · "Make the turn more surprising." · "Cut it to 36 seconds."

## Exercise
Write one rule that passes all four tests and one misfit trait that's its blind spot. Ask Claude for the full Fable
story, then check: does the last line echo the first line or the ritual?
