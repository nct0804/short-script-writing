# Lesson 2 · Writing a Fact script

A Fact script makes the viewer ask **"why would he do that?"** in sentence 1 and pays it off with something real.
Worked example throughout: *The Cook Who Spun Every Egg* (full script and notes:
`.claude/skills/fact-script/references/examples.md`; the animated version: `samples/fact-the-cook-who-spun-every-egg/`).

## 1. Start from a mechanism, then find the deed
Good Fact topics have four things (score each 1-5):
1. **A paradox in one line:** it sounds wrong and it's true.
2. **Stakes + people:** someone did something, and something was at risk.
3. **Visual cause and effect:** you can *show* it.
4. **One idea to explain it.**

Then find **the deed**: the moment someone does the thing that only makes sense once you know the mechanism.
> Mechanism: a raw egg's liquid drags when it spins, a boiled egg's solid inside doesn't.
> Deed: **"He spun every egg in the diner before he cracked a single one."**

Past tense, no name, no reason. The reason is the payoff.

## 2. The six beats (Fact story, ~60 s, ~220 words)
| Beat | The egg script | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | He spun every egg in the diner before he cracked a single one. | the deed, 13 words, picturable |
| 2. Setup | You see, it's his first shift, and someone dumped the boiled eggs and the raw eggs into the same crate. And the lunch rush wants egg salad in twenty minutes. | the problem and a deadline, in present tense |
| 3. Escalation | So he spins … The cooks stop flipping pancakes to stare … But he doesn't look up … Then the boss storms in … So he grabs one more egg, stops it dead with one finger, lets go, and it starts turning again. Raw. | So / But / Then chains every line to the last; the mechanism is *shown* before it's explained |
| 4. Mystery | But how does a spin tell you what's inside? | the viewer's question, out loud |
| 5. Reveal | Well, a boiled egg is solid, so the shell and the inside turn together … | one mechanism, plain words; "In real life, chefs really check eggs this way." says what's real |
| 6. Payoff | … until it slowly rises onto its tip and keeps spinning, standing straight up. | a true, surprising, visual last line. Nothing after it |

## 3. The truth rules
- The **mechanism** is true and sourced (two good sources; never "a viral post said").
- The **story around it** can be invented, if it's generic: an unnamed cook in an unnamed diner. Mark those lines
  **dramatized** in the facts table, and say what's real in the reveal.
- A **Fact event** (a real date and place) is sourced in every detail. Never invent details about real people.
- Jokes must be true details too, or clearly the narrator's exaggeration ("like a card dealer").

## 4. Sound like the viral ones
- **3.7 words per second**, almost no pauses. A 60 s script is about 220 words.
- **Long-short rhythm:** one run-on sentence that speeds up ("he just spins, watches, and drops each egg into one of
  two bowls, faster and faster, like a card dealer"), then a one-word jab ("Raw.").
- **Present tense** after the hook. **Roles, not names** ("the boss"). **Unattributed lines** for the other characters.
- **Never narrate the picture.** The voice adds why it's strange; the picture shows what happens.

## 5. Ask Claude
- "Fact script about [topic]." → it researches, proposes 3 deed hooks, writes, sources and scores.
- "Pitch me 6 Fact ideas about [kitchens / ships / the human body]."
- "Here's my draft. Doctor it." → line-numbered diagnosis, then the tightened version.
- "Make it 40 seconds." · "Give me 3 more hooks." · "Write it as an 'If you …' explainer."

## Exercise
Pick a trick you know from a job or a hobby (a mechanic's, a baker's, a sailor's). Write its deed in one sentence,
past tense, no reason. Then ask Claude for the full Fact story and compare its score card with your instinct.
