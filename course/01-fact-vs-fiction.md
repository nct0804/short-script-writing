# Lesson 1 · Fact vs Fiction

Two script types, built as opposites. Every choice in one is the reverse of the other, and that's what keeps them from
blurring into a vague "story video".

| | **Fact** | **Fiction** |
|---|---|---|
| The idea | something real: a mechanism, a trick people really use, a true event | something invented: a world with one impossible rule |
| The question it plants | *Why would he do that?* | *What does this rule cost, and who pays?* |
| Narrator | an adult storyteller about an unnamed "he" | a kid (7-12) in the first person |
| Sentence 1 | the strange deed, past tense: "He spun every egg in the diner before he cracked a single one." | the rule: "In my town, if you hum, flowers grow where you stand." |
| Middle | So / But / Then escalation; people react; the mechanism is planted where we can see it | funny proofs of the rule; "Me?" (the narrator doesn't fit); a loved one with a ritual; pressure; "That's when I understood." |
| Ending | the real reason, then a twist or a visual payoff that shows it | the first line or the ritual comes back, changed |
| Length | about 60 s | about 2 min (story) or 36 s (gag) |
| Pace | 3.7 words/s, sentences ~14 words (long-short rhythm) | 4.3 words/s (gag 5.2), sentences ~6 words |
| Truth | every fact sourced; the invented framing is marked "dramatized" | fully invented, and fully original |
| Captions | one bold word at a time | 2-4 words, or one big word (eerie), or top-of-frame chunks (gag) |
| Feels like | "Huh, I didn't know that. I'm sending this to someone." | "Wait, what would I do? … Oh no. Oh." |

## Which one is my idea?
Ask one question: **does it really happen?**
- **Yes** (science, history, a trade trick, a "why does X", a weird law) → **Fact**.
  - It happened once, with a date and a place → **Fact event**.
  - It happens every time, and a person can act it out → **Fact story** (the default).
  - No person in it at all ("If you fell into Jupiter …") → **Fact explainer**.
- **No** (a "what if", a magic rule, a feeling made into a law) → **Fiction**.
  - It has a family heart (a sibling, a grandparent) → **Fiction story**, warm or eerie.
  - It's a crush, a bet or a joke → **Fiction gag** (36 s).

## The same seed, both ways
Seed: **eggs**.
- **Fact:** "He spun every egg in the diner before he cracked a single one." (A spin tells raw from boiled; the payoff is
  a boiled egg rising onto its tip. See `samples/fact-the-cook-who-spun-every-egg/`.)
- **Fiction:** "In my town, every egg hatches into whatever you were thinking when you cracked it." (The kid who can't
  stop worrying; a little sister's birthday cake; the turn: the eggs never hatched thoughts, they hatched wishes.)

Seed: **laughing**.
- **Fact:** a real reason laughter is contagious, acted out by someone whose laugh empties a theatre and then fills it.
  (Research the mechanism first.)
- **Fiction:** "Every time my crush laughs, flowers grow wherever she looks." (A gag: thirty days of failed jokes. See
  `samples/fiction-my-crushs-laugh-grows-flowers/`.)

## Exercise

sentence 1 only. Then ask Claude: "Here are five first lines, Fact or Fiction each. Score the hooks and tell me which
